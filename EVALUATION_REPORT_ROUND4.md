### 2.2 Safety Implications of SegFormer Frame Skipping (`seg_frame_skip: 1`)
- **Mechanism**: With `seg_frame_skip: 1`, SegFormer deep neural inference is executed every second frame. On interleaved frames, the system reuses the cached segmentation mask while evaluating lightweight per-frame classical cues (Sobel gradient jump, brightness step, and temporal consistency).
- **Latency & Safety Impact**:
  - Skipping every second frame reduces SegFormer load from ~30 ms/frame to ~15.8 ms/frame, allowing the entire pipeline to exceed 15 FPS on CPU.
  - **Drop-off & Ledge Protection**: Sudden loss of ground surface is caught on every frame by the fast classical gradient and surface consistency checks (`_gradient_jump_check`), which bypass smoothing history and force immediate `UNKNOWN` or `BLOCKED` states without waiting for deep segmentation refresh.
  - **Trade-off**: In dynamic scenarios, a sudden transition onto clear floor may experience up to 1 frame (~63 ms) delay before the deep segmentation model confirms navigable terrain. For safety-critical stopping (drop-offs/obstacles), classical vetoes execute on every frame with zero frame skip.

### 3.2 Calibration Diagnosis & Findings
- **Assessment**: The freespace confidence score is **partially calibrated but exhibits conservatism**:
  - In the lowest bin `[0.0, 0.2)`, the mean confidence is 0.0000 while observed accuracy is 71.60%. This is because all `UNKNOWN` corridor decisions are strictly clamped to 0.00 confidence for safety, even when identifying a hazardous scene correctly.
  - In the high-confidence bin `[0.8, 1.0)`, when the system claims ~99% confidence, observed accuracy is 77.33% (a 22% overconfidence gap on static single-image evaluations).
  - Consequently, the confidence score represents an evidence-derived ordinal ranking rather than a calibrated posterior probability.

## 4. Removal of Fabricated and Assumed Values (Step 7 Audit)

In compliance with the anti-fabrication mandate, all hardcoded assumptions and fabricated metrics have been replaced with evidence-backed derivations:

1. **Continuous Evidence-Based Freespace Confidence** (`spatialvector/freespace/corridor_estimator.py`):
   - Eliminated constants (`0.95`, `0.85`, `0.80`, `0.10`, `0.0`).
   - Derived confidence as a continuous function combining SegFormer ground fraction margin ($\Delta g$), per-pixel softmax probability distribution, classical cue agreement bonus, and temporal consistency. `UNKNOWN` decisions strictly yield `0.00`.
2. **Pinhole Ground-Plane Pothole Distance & Provenance** (`spatialvector/hazards/ground_hazard.py`):
   - Eliminated linear bounding-box scaling ($5.0\,\text{m} \to 0.4\,\text{m}$).
   - Pothole distance is computed via optical pinhole flat-ground geometry ($Z = f_y \cdot H_{\text{cam}} / \Delta y$) using camera mounting height ($1.30\,\text{m}$) and pitch ($15^\circ$).
   - If optical parameters or camera mounting geometry are unconfigured, distance returns `dist_m = None` and `dist_provenance = "unknown"`.
3. **Pinhole Object Distance & Truncation Handling** (`run_camera_prediction_viewer.py`):
   - Object distance estimation uses config-driven camera focal length ratio (`focal_length_ratio: 0.72`) and class real-world heights from `default.yaml`.
   - Bounding boxes touching image boundaries (truncated boxes) and classes with unconfigured heights return `dist_m = None` and display as `"unknown"` on the AR overlay, preventing false measurements.
4. **IMU Sensor Provenance Disambiguation** (`spatialvector/motion/imu_reader.py`, `run_camera_prediction_viewer.py`):
   - Replaced boolean simulation flags with explicit status tags (`status: "SIMULATED"` vs `"HARDWARE"`).
   - In the live viewer HUD, the sensor status is rendered as `IMU / Gyro: SIMULATED` with an amber warning dot.
5. **No Synthetic TTC Overrides for Stationary Objects** (`spatialvector/decision/prediction.py`):
   - Objects without approach velocity (`v_approach <= 0`) return `ttc_s = None`. No synthetic collision time is fabricated for stationary obstacles; the risk engine acts on spatial corridor occupation and `proximity_risk`.

## 6. What Could Not Be Verified (Known Limitations & Hardware Bounds)

The following items cannot be validated within the current codebase or software simulation environment and require physical hardware testing:

1. **Physical IMU Sensor & Angular Velocity Compensation**:
   - The MPU-6050 serial interface (`IMUReader` in `imu_reader.py`) cannot be tested without physical hardware connected to a serial port. All current tests run with `SimulatedIMUReader`.
   - Dynamic body bounce, torso pitch oscillations, and head turning during walking cannot be simulated realistically with synthetic sinusoids.
2. **Physical Haptic Feedback & Belt Integration**:
   - Arduino motor driver PWM signals cannot be verified without physical motors and an Arduino microcontroller connected via USB serial.
   - User comprehension of vibrotactile directional cues (frequency, duty cycle, urgency levels) requires human walking experiments.
3. **Monocular Scale Invariance Under Camera Pitch Dynamics**:
   - Distance estimation via pinhole geometry assumes a rigid camera mounting height and pitch relative to flat ground. In walking scenarios, natural gait induces pitch changes that alter perceived distances unless dynamically stabilized by physical IMU fusion.
4. **Real User Walking Footage**:
   - The repository currently contains no physical camera recordings made with `--record-session` by walking users. All evaluation fixtures consist of public web imagery and synthetic clips.
